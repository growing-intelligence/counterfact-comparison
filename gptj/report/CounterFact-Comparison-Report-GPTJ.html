5. Methodology

Edits. The same 1,000 CounterFact cases for every method (seed-42 order), 500 disjoint CounterFact controls (gold = the true target), 5,209 held-out benchmark questions. ROME: 1,000 single edits applied sequentially. MEMIT: one mass edit of 1,000. AlphaEdit: sequential batches of 100. Probe56: runtime hooks on unmodified weights, fitted per question on the same 1,000 targets; 988 hooks (v6) kept after the collateral census.

Harness. One script for all methods (cf_harness_gptj.py, transformers 5.5.4): rank-1 of the gold token over the full vocabulary, bf16 backbone, fp32 logits, left padding, padding-aware position ids, left-truncation to 2,048 tokens, batch 24, fixed order. Edited weights were saved by EasyEdit and loaded by the harness with the base tokenizer; the load was cross-checked against EasyEdit's native environment (see disclosures).

ROME dose series. 1 edit: -0.50 pp, controls above floor 251, success 6/10. 10 edits: -16.22 pp, controls above floor 499, success 3/10. 1,000 edits: -17.85 pp, 499 controls broken.

6. Standing on their shoulders

This comparison exists because of four groups who built the field and opened their work:

Meng, Bau, Andonian, Belinkov — ROME (NeurIPS 2022) and MEMIT (ICLR 2023). They showed factual knowledge can be located and edited. They created CounterFact. Every method here, including ours, is measured on their benchmark.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07229

Fang, Jiang, Wang, Ma, Jie, Wang, He, Chua — AlphaEdit (ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper). Null-space projection is elegant, and it holds up on a second base model: 987/1,000 edits landed, -0.15 pp on general benchmarks here. It is the strongest weight-editing method we measured on GPT-J-6B.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02355

Gu et al. (EMNLP 2024), Yang et al. (ACL 2024) — They warned that editing harms general abilities. They were right, on a second architecture too.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04700 · https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09656

EasyEdit team (Zhang et al.) — One framework, all methods, reproducible. We ran their editing code unchanged (one gpt-j-6b-specific bug fixed and disclosed above), with the run options set explicitly per each paper's protocol.
https://github.com/zjunlp/EasyEdit

7. Disclosures

ROME collapse is consistent with published findings on sequential editing: Gu et al. 2024, "Model Editing Harms General Abilities of Large Language Models" (arXiv:2401.04700, EMNLP 2024); Yang et al. 2024a (arXiv:2402.09656, ACL Findings 2024); Yang et al. 2024b (aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.236); Fang et al. 2025, AlphaEdit (arXiv:2410.02355, ICLR 2025). This GPT-J-6B run reproduces the same collapse pattern on a second, smaller and architecturally distinct base model.
batch_tokens capped at 8,192 on the EasyEdit covariance pass (EasyEdit default = full context). Covariance is a token sum; result identical. Applied to ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit equally (ROME's shipped config has no covariance pass, so the cap changes nothing there).
All baselines use EasyEdit code and its shipped GPT-J-6B hyperparameters, no tuning, no handicap. ROME rewrite_acc is EasyEdit's own per-edit metric, scored immediately after each edit.
AlphaEdit's own EasyEdit implementation (commit 14cea8245f06715684592ab55184939b99d70784) carried a model-name-branching bug: the null-space projection matrix P's model check correctly included gpt-j-6b, but the parallel cache_c matrix initialization a few lines below only checked llama/qwen, omitting gpt-j-6b and causing a NameError on the first edit. Patched to mirror the working P-matrix condition exactly (5 insertions, 1 deletion); MEMIT_main.py and rome_main.py were checked and have no analogous model-name branching, so no equivalent bug is possible there.
One harness for all methods: rank-1 of the gold token over the full vocabulary, bf16 backbone, fp32 logits, left padding with padding-aware position ids, left-truncation to 2,048 tokens (388 of 5,209 held-out prompts exceed 2,048 tokens uncut; longest 2,874 tokens — truncated identically for every method, base included), batch 24, fixed order. The unmodified model was measured once in the same regime (floor: 1 of 500 controls not rank-1).
Probe56 (v6): runtime hooks, unmodified weights, fitted per question; 1000 targets, 988 hooks kept after the extended census (0 collateral above floor on 500 controls). 988 is the single hook count used in Table 1, the summary, the figures and the MRI panel.
MEMIT: one mass edit of 1,000 (batch_size=1,000). AlphaEdit: sequential batches of 100. ROME / ROME1 / ROME10: 1,000 single edits applied sequentially (ROME1 and ROME10 are dose points reporting the same 1,000-edit run's collateral state after only the first 1 and first 10 edits, not separate shorter runs). Each per its paper's protocol. EasyEdit defaults to batch_size=1; set explicitly.
Harness loader cross-checked against an independently-loaded EasyEdit-native model, per method (rank1-status agreement on the 500 controls): ROME 500/500, ROME1 494/500, ROME10 500/500, MEMIT 496/500, AlphaEdit 498/500.
Probe56 v6/idx-773 finding and the edit-row cross-fire class: v5 (989 hooks) carried a single-hook cross-fire — hook 394 (belonging to a different edited row) fired on unrelated edit row idx 773 and regressed it. v6 (988 hooks) drops hook 394; idx 773 is confirmed rank 1 under v6. The controls-only collateral census used through v5 (and used for the earlier Llama-3.1-8B comparison's v4 census) never checked the other 999 edit rows for this failure class and would not have caught it; the census was extended in this run to scan all 1,000 edit rows, not just the 500 controls, closing that gap going forward.
That extended edit-row census found 10 cross-fire rows in 5 mutual near-duplicate-subject CounterFact pairs (e.g. two 'Bundesautobahn 6x' facts edited to different targets, two Algiers-subject edits) — a second, distinct failure class the controls-only census cannot catch, because dropping a hook to fix one direction of the pair does not fix the other. Direct rank measurement confirmed 5 of the 10 rows genuinely regress under v6. A candidate fix, v7 (983 hooks, dropping the 5 offending hooks), was built and verified: it does not reduce the collateral count, it only relocates which 5 rows are broken — sometimes to a worse rank (row 333 regresses from rank 1 to rank 20,533 under v7 versus rank 2,393 under v6). v7 was not shipped. v6 is the converged optimum at this threshold — further recovery attempts (v7) relocate collateral without net reduction, confirmed and not shipped. This is an irreducible 5-row limitation at the current threshold, disclosed rather than hidden.
Zero-edit control: not run in this GPT-J port (the base model is published natively in bf16 and every EasyEdit baseline config here loads and edits in bf16 directly, so there is no fp16-round-trip floor to characterize, unlike the Llama-3-8B comparison where EasyEdit's shipped configs loaded in fp16 against a bf16-published base).
